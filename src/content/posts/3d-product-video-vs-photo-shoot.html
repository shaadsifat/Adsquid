Short answer: choose a photo or video shoot when you need real people, places and emotion. Choose 3D when your product itself is the star, when you need many angles, colours or updates, or when you want ads that look different from everything else in the feed. For many brands the smart answer is both: 3D for product visuals, shoots for lifestyle.

As a creative advisor, I help brands decide how to spend their production budget. The question "3D or shoot?" comes up more every year, because 3D has become faster and more realistic. Here is how I think about it.

When a shoot is the better choice

  • People and emotion matter: fashion on a model, food being enjoyed, a family in a new home.
  • The place sells: restaurants, hotels, clinics, showrooms and events.
  • Testimonials and stories: real customers and team members on camera.
  • Very fast, simple content: phone-shot Reels and behind-the-scenes videos.

When 3D is the better choice

  • The product is the hero: electronics, cosmetics, bottles, packaging, furniture and gadgets.
  • You need impossible shots: exploded views, inside a device, products assembling themselves, liquid splashes.
  • Many variants: one model can show every colour and size without a new shoot.
  • Pre-launch: visuals ready before the physical product or packaging exists.
  • Standing out in feeds: stylised CGI ads stop the scroll because they look different.

Cost over time, not just the first project

A single 3D video can cost about the same as a well-planned shoot. The difference shows up over time. Once the 3D model exists, every new angle, colour, still image or seasonal ad is far cheaper than another shoot day. A shoot is often cheaper for one quick campaign, while 3D usually wins for products you will market for years.

Realism: what makes 3D look premium

The gap between cheap and premium 3D is large. Three things decide quality:

  1. Accurate modelling: correct proportions, edges and small details like seams, buttons and labels.
  2. Real materials: glass that refracts, metal that reflects, fabric and plastic with the right texture.
  3. Lighting and camera: studio-style lighting and smooth, purposeful camera moves.

Ask for a still render of your product before approving a full animation. If the still looks real, the video will too.

The hybrid approach most brands should use

For most product brands, the best plan combines both. Use 3D for product hero visuals, ads, packaging and marketplace images. Use shoots for people, places and stories. The 3D model becomes a long-term asset, and the shoots bring warmth and trust.

Questions to ask before you decide

  • Is the product or the person the hero of this content?
  • Will we need new angles, colours or seasonal versions later?
  • Does the product exist physically yet?
  • Do we want ads that look different from competitors?
  • How long will we keep marketing this product?
