How Facebook charges you

Meta runs an auction for every ad slot. You do not pay a fixed price. You pay what it takes to win that slot against other advertisers who want the same people.

Three things decide what you pay:

  • Your objective. Asking for sales or leads costs more per impression than asking for reach. It is usually cheaper per sale.
  • Your audience. Small, popular audiences (Dhaka, 25-34, high income) cost more than broad ones.
  • Your ad quality. Ads that people watch, click and do not hide get cheaper delivery. Weak ads pay a penalty.

How much budget do you need?

Start from your goal, not from a round number. Work backwards:

  1. Decide how many sales or leads you want per month.
  2. Estimate a realistic cost per result from the table above.
  3. Multiply. That is your starting budget.

Example: a fashion page wants 300 inbox orders a month. If a message costs ৳20 and one in four messages becomes an order, each order costs about ৳80 in ads. 300 orders need roughly ৳24,000 in ad spend.

Give any new campaign at least two to four weeks before judging it. Meta needs data to learn who buys.

Five ways to lower your cost per sale

1. Fix tracking first

Install the Meta Pixel and the Conversions API, and test every event. Without them, Facebook optimizes for the wrong people and your reports guess.

2. Use Ads Manager, not Boost

Boost is built for reach. Ads Manager lets you choose sales or messages as the goal, test audiences and control placements.

3. Refresh creatives every two to three weeks

The same ad shown again and again gets more expensive. Plan new hooks, angles and formats on a schedule.

4. Let video do the selling

Short vertical videos with a strong first second usually beat static images on cost per result. See our video content service.

5. Retarget warm people

People who watched your video or visited your site are cheaper to convert. A small retargeting budget often brings the best return in the account.
