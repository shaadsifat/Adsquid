Short answer: choose Google Ads when people already search for what you sell, and Facebook (Meta) ads when you need to create demand with visuals. Most growing businesses in Bangladesh end up using both, but the order matters. Start where your buyer's intent is strongest, then expand.

Every week someone asks us the same question: "Should I put my budget into Google or Facebook?" The honest answer depends on three things: how people look for your product, what your margins allow and how good your tracking is. This guide breaks it down so you can decide before you spend a single taka.

The core difference: intent vs discovery

Google Ads shows your ad to people who type a search. Someone searching "AC repair Mirpur" or "buy laptop Dhaka" already has a need. Your job is to be the best answer at that moment.

Facebook and Instagram ads show your ad to people who were not looking for you. They are scrolling, chatting and watching Reels. Your job is to stop the scroll and create interest. That is why creatives matter so much more on Meta.

When Google Ads should come first

Start with Google when the search demand already exists. A quick test: type your product or service into Google with a location. If you see ads and competitors there, people are searching and buying.

  • Urgent services: repairs, clinics, diagnostic tests, rentals and travel bookings.
  • High-value decisions: software, machinery, real estate, education and B2B services.
  • Exports: buyers abroad who search for suppliers, manufacturers or wholesale products.
  • Local businesses: people searching "near me" or with an area name, where Maps and call ads shine.

When Facebook ads should come first

Start with Meta when your product sells with its looks, its story or its price, and when people do not yet know they want it.

  • F-commerce and e-commerce: clothes, cosmetics, home decor, gadgets and food.
  • Inbox-driven sales: businesses that close orders in Messenger or WhatsApp.
  • New products: anything people would not think to search for yet.
  • Events and offers: Eid collections, flash sales and launches that need reach fast.

With Meta ads, the creative is the targeting. A strong first second in a Reel often matters more than any interest you select.

A simple decision framework

  1. Check search demand. Search your product with a location. Ads and competitors mean demand exists.
  2. Check your visuals. Do you have strong photos or videos? If not, Meta ads will struggle until you fix that.
  3. Check your margin. Know the most you can pay for one sale and still profit. This is your target cost per sale.
  4. Fix tracking first. Install GA4, Google tags, the Meta Pixel and Conversions API, and test a real purchase or lead.
  5. Start with one platform. Put 70-80 percent of the budget where intent or fit is strongest.
  6. Test the second with 20-30 percent. After four to six weeks, move budget to the lower cost per sale.

How the two platforms work together

The best results usually come from using both in their strongest role. Meta ads create awareness and first visits. Google Search catches people when they later search your brand or product. Retargeting on both platforms brings back visitors who did not buy the first time.

A practical pattern for many Bangladeshi brands: Meta for discovery and inbox orders, Google Search for brand and high-intent keywords, and YouTube or Display for retargeting. Each platform has a job, and the reports show which one pays.

Common mistakes we see

  • Boosting posts instead of running proper campaigns in Ads Manager.
  • Bidding on broad keywords like "shoes" without negative keywords.
  • Judging a platform after one week and a tiny budget.
  • Sending Google traffic to a Facebook page with no clear way to buy.
  • Running the same creative for months until costs double.
