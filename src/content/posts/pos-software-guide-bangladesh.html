A point of sale system is the heart of a shop or restaurant. When it works, sales are quick and the numbers are trustworthy. When it does not, queues grow, stock goes missing and nobody believes the reports. As a tech specialist who builds and configures POS systems, here is how I help owners choose.

Features by business type

  • Retail and supermarkets: barcode labels, variants (size and colour), purchase orders and supplier management.
  • Restaurants and cafes: table management, kitchen order tickets, split bills, modifiers and delivery orders.
  • Pharmacies: batch numbers, expiry date alerts and generic name search.
  • Fashion and electronics: variants, serial numbers, warranty tracking and exchanges.

Inventory: where POS pays for itself

The biggest benefit of POS is not faster billing. It is knowing exactly what you have. With every sale and purchase recorded, you can see slow-moving stock, reorder before items run out and spot missing stock early. Low-stock alerts and simple purchase orders turn guesswork into a routine.

Multi-branch and online selling

If you run several branches, choose a POS with a central dashboard where you can see each branch's sales, transfer stock and set prices in one place. If you also sell online, connect the POS to your store so stock stays the same in both places and you never sell something you do not have.

Questions to ask any POS provider

  1. Does billing keep working offline, and how does it sync?
  2. Which payment methods are supported, including bKash?
  3. Can it calculate VAT correctly and print the receipts we need?
  4. Which hardware models are supported?
  5. How are data backups handled, and can we export our data?
  6. Can it connect to our online store, accounting or ERP?
  7. What does support look like after launch, and how fast do they respond?

Ready-made vs custom POS

For a standard shop, a well-chosen ready-made POS is often enough. A custom or customised POS makes sense when you have special pricing rules, unusual workflows, many branches or need deep integration with your e-commerce, accounting or ERP. The key is to test with your real products and your real cashiers before deciding.

A simple selection checklist

  • Fast billing tested at your counter
  • Offline mode confirmed
  • Cash, card and bKash supported
  • Inventory, alerts and purchase orders included
  • VAT and receipts correct
  • Hardware compatibility confirmed
  • Branch and online store sync if needed
  • Backups, data export and support agreed
