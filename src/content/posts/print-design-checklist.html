Short answer: before you send any design to a printer, check six things: the exact size, 3 mm bleed, safe margins, CMYK colour, 300 DPI images and embedded or outlined fonts. Export a print-ready PDF and ask for a proof. These checks prevent the most expensive printing mistakes.

Printing mistakes are painful because they cost twice: once for the bad print and again for the reprint. Over the years our design team has prepared files for flyers, brochures, business cards, menus, packaging and billboards for printers across Dhaka. This is the checklist we run every time.

1. Confirm the exact size first

Ask the printer for the final trimmed size before you start designing. Common sizes include A4 (210 x 297 mm), A5 (148 x 210 mm) and business cards, which in Bangladesh are often 3.5 x 2 inches. Designing at the wrong size and scaling later often blurs text and images.

2. Add bleed and keep a safe margin

AreaWhat it isTypical value
BleedBackground extended past the cut line3 mm on each side
Trim lineWhere the paper is cutThe final size
Safe marginArea inside the trim kept free of text4-5 mm from the edge

Cutting machines are never perfectly precise. Without bleed you risk thin white lines on the edges. Without a safe margin, a phone number can be cut in half.

3. Design in CMYK, not RGB

Screens mix light (RGB), printers mix ink (CMYK). Bright screen colours like electric violet or neon green often look duller in print. Set your document to CMYK from the start and check how brand colours convert. For exact brand colours on large runs, ask about spot colours.

Always ask for a printed proof of colour-critical jobs, such as packaging or brand stationery. A proof costs little compared with a full reprint.

4. Use high-resolution images

Images need 300 DPI at the size they will be printed. A photo downloaded from Facebook may look sharp on a phone but will print blurry on an A4 flyer. Use original photos or high-resolution stock, and keep logos as vector files (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF).

5. Handle fonts properly

  • Embed fonts in the PDF or convert text to outlines for logos and headlines.
  • Check Bangla text carefully; some fonts break conjuncts when exported badly.
  • Avoid tiny text: body text under about 7 pt becomes hard to read.

6. Watch rich black and thin lines

Large black areas print deeper with a "rich black" mix, while small black text should stay 100 percent black only, to avoid blurry edges. Very thin lines under 0.25 pt may disappear in print.

7. Choose paper and finish with the printer

ItemCommon paperFinish ideas
Flyers100-130 gsm art paperGloss or matte
Brochures130-170 gsm, heavier coverMatte lamination on cover
Business cards300-350 gsm cardMatte lamination, spot UV, foil
Menus250-300 gsmLamination for durability

Paper and finish change how colours look and how premium the piece feels. Decide them before final colour checks.

8. Export a print-ready PDF

  1. Export as PDF with crop marks and bleed included.
  2. Keep colour in CMYK and images at full resolution.
  3. Embed or outline all fonts.
  4. Name files clearly: brochure-cover-A4-v3-print.pdf.
  5. Send one final file, and confirm the version number with the printer.

The checklist in one place

  • Correct final size confirmed with the printer
  • 3 mm bleed on all sides
  • Text and logos inside the safe margin
  • CMYK colour, proof for brand colours
  • 300 DPI images, vector logos
  • Fonts embedded or outlined, Bangla checked
  • Paper and finish agreed
  • Print-ready PDF with crop marks
