Short answer: a Reel succeeds or fails in its first second. Open with movement, a bold statement or a visual surprise, put text on screen immediately, deliver the value fast and end with a clear next step. Plan the hook before you film anything, and test two or three hooks for every idea.

As a creative director, I review every Reel script before our team films it. The question I ask is always the same: "Why would someone stop scrolling here?" If we cannot answer it in one sentence, we do not shoot yet. Here is the framework we use for brands across Bangladesh.

Why the first second decides everything

People scroll Reels with their thumb already moving. The platform watches what they do: if they stop, watch to the end, replay or share, it shows the Reel to more people. If they swipe away in the first second, reach stops. Your hook is not part of the video. It is the reason the rest of the video gets watched at all.

A simple 30-second structure

  1. 0-1 second, hook: movement and on-screen text that state the promise.
  2. 1-5 seconds, context: why it matters to the viewer.
  3. 5-25 seconds, value: the steps, the demo, the story or the reveal.
  4. 25-30 seconds, action: follow, message us, order now or visit the link.

Write the on-screen hook text first, then plan the shots that support it. Most weak Reels are filmed first and "fixed" in editing.

Filming tips that make a big difference

  • Start in motion: the camera or subject should already be moving in frame one.
  • Use natural light: face a window or shoot outside in shade; avoid harsh overhead light.
  • Frame for 9:16: keep the subject centred and away from the top and bottom edges, where buttons cover the screen.
  • Record clean sound: a small clip-on microphone is the best upgrade for talking Reels.
  • Shoot extra: film each shot two or three times and grab close-ups for cutaways.

Editing for pace

Cut every pause, breath and repeated word. Change the shot every one to three seconds, using close-ups, angle changes or text pop-ups. Add captions for every spoken word, because many people watch with the sound off. Keep brand colours and fonts consistent so viewers start to recognise your Reels.

Sound: trending audio or voice-over?

Trending audio can give a short-term reach boost, especially for fun or lifestyle content. For tutorials, products and ads, a clear voice-over usually explains better and builds more trust. Whichever you choose, keep music low under speech.

What to measure after posting

MetricWhat it tells you
3-second view rateDid the hook work?
Average watch timeDid the middle hold attention?
Shares and savesWas it useful or entertaining enough to keep?
Profile visits and messagesDid it move people toward buying?

If the 3-second rate is low, change the hook. If people drop in the middle, tighten the edit. If they watch but do not act, make the call to action clearer.

Batch your Reels to stay consistent

Plan eight to twelve Reels at once, film them in one or two shoot days and edit in a batch. Batching keeps quality high and costs low, and it means you never post in a rush.
