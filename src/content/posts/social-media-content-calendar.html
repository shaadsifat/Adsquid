Short answer: a content calendar is a shared plan of what you will post, where and when. Plan one month at a time: start with key dates and offers, fill the rest with your content pillars, assign formats, write captions, get approvals in one round and schedule everything in advance. Leave about 20 percent open for trends.

As a content specialist, I see the same pattern in many teams: great ideas on Saturday, panic on Wednesday. A calendar turns social media from a daily emergency into a calm monthly routine. Here is the exact process we use for client pages.

Step 1: Mark the dates that matter

Before any post ideas, open the month and mark the dates that will shape your content. In Bangladesh these often drive the biggest spikes in attention and sales.

  • Festivals and national days: Eid, Pohela Boishakh, Victory Day, Independence Day, International Mother Language Day and Durga Puja.
  • Shopping moments: month-end salary days, winter collections, back to school, 11.11 and 12.12 sales.
  • Your own dates: launches, restocks, events, anniversaries and planned offers.

Plan festival content at least two to three weeks ahead. Design, photography and ad approvals always take longer around Eid.

Step 2: Fill the month with your pillars

With key dates in place, fill the remaining slots from your content pillars: product, proof, education, behind the scenes and community. Spread them evenly so no week becomes all sales posts.

Step 4: Build the calendar sheet

A simple Google Sheet is enough. Use one row per post and these columns:

  1. Date and time: when it goes live.
  2. Platform: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn.
  3. Pillar: product, proof, education, behind the scenes or community.
  4. Format: Reel, carousel, image or Story.
  5. Hook or headline: the first line people will see.
  6. Caption: full text, in Bangla, English or both.
  7. Visual brief: what the designer or video editor needs.
  8. Call to action: message us, order now, book a call.
  9. Status: idea, in design, ready for approval, approved, scheduled.

Step 5: Write captions that start conversations

Good captions follow a simple shape: a hook, the value and a clear next step. Keep the first line short, because platforms cut it off. Ask a question when you want comments. Mention price and delivery details when you want orders, so people do not have to ask.

Step 6: Approve everything in one round

Approval delays kill calendars. Share the full month with the decision maker at once, collect all comments in one place and agree on a deadline. One round of changes for the whole month is far faster than daily back-and-forth.

Step 7: Schedule, then stay flexible

Schedule approved posts in Meta Business Suite or your preferred tool. Keep about 20 percent of slots open for trends, customer moments and quick news. Review the calendar every Thursday so next week is always ready.

Step 8: Learn from the month

At month end, sort posts by messages, saves and shares, not just likes. Note which pillars and formats worked best, then give them more space next month. Over three months, the calendar becomes sharper and the results more predictable.
