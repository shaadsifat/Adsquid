Short answer: a post that sells has one message, one clear focal point, short readable text, consistent branding and a reason to act. Design for a phone screen first, test the post at thumbnail size and make two or three variations so you can see which one people respond to.

Every day I review dozens of social posts from brands across Bangladesh. The ones that work are rarely the most decorated. They are the clearest. Here are the nine rules our design team follows for every post, carousel and ad creative.

1. One post, one message

Before opening Figma or Photoshop, write the message in one sentence: "Eid collection, 20 percent off until Friday." If you cannot say it in one line, the design will feel crowded. Every extra message weakens the main one.

2. Build a clear visual hierarchy

People should see things in this order: the image or product, the headline, then the call to action. Use size, contrast and space to create that order.

LevelElementHow to make it stand out
1Product or main imageLargest element, clean background
2HeadlineBold type, high contrast, short
3Offer or detailSmaller text or a badge
4Call to action and logoButton shape or corner placement

3. Design for the phone, not the monitor

Most people see your post on a phone, often for less than a second. Zoom out to thumbnail size before you finish. If the headline is unreadable or the product disappears, simplify.

4. Keep text short and readable

  • Headlines under about seven words.
  • No more than two fonts per post.
  • Strong contrast between text and background.
  • Bangla text set with a proper Bangla font, with enough line height.

Put prices, sizes and delivery details in the caption or a carousel slide, not crammed onto the first image.

5. Stay consistent with your brand

Use the same colours, fonts, logo position and photo style across posts. Consistency turns a feed into a brand. When people recognise your post before they read your name, your ads also get cheaper, because familiarity builds trust.

6. Use the right size for every placement

PlacementSizeRatio
Facebook and Instagram feed1080 x 1350 px4:5
Square post1080 x 1080 px1:1
Stories and Reels cover1080 x 1920 px9:16
LinkedIn feed1200 x 1200 or 1080 x 1350 px1:1 or 4:5

For Stories, keep text away from the top and bottom 250 pixels, where profile names and buttons cover the design.

7. Make carousels that people swipe

The first slide must promise something worth swiping for: "5 mistakes that waste your ad budget". Each slide covers one point, and the last slide gives the call to action. Carousels are saved and shared more than single posts, which helps reach.

8. Show real people and real products

Stock photos look like stock photos. Real product shots, customer photos and team faces build more trust. If you use stock imagery, edit it to match your brand colours and style.

9. Always make variations for ads

For any post you plan to boost or run as an ad, make two or three versions: a different headline, a different image or a different colour. Ad platforms will show which version people respond to, and you will learn faster than any opinion in a meeting.

A quick pre-publish checklist

  1. Can I state the message in one sentence?
  2. Is the product or main image the first thing I see?
  3. Is the headline readable at thumbnail size?
  4. Are brand colours, fonts and logo consistent?
  5. Is the size right for the placement?
  6. Is the next step clear in the design or caption?
