Short answer: a social media strategy is a simple plan that answers five questions: what you want social media to do for the business, who you are talking to, which platforms they use, what you will post and how you will measure it. Write it on one page, follow it for three months, then improve it with real data.

Many small businesses in Bangladesh post every day and still feel stuck. The problem is rarely effort. It is direction. Without a strategy, posts become random, results become random and the team burns out. As a brand strategist, I start every client with the same one-page plan below.

Step 1: Pick one main business goal

"Grow on social media" is not a goal. Choose the one outcome that matters most for the next three months.

More inbox ordersMessages, orders from socialProducts, prices, offers, reviews
More bookings or callsCalls, WhatsApp chats, form leadsBefore and after, FAQs, trust
Brand awareness for a launchReach, video views, new followersReels, teasers, behind the scenes
Hiring or employer brandApplications, LinkedIn followersTeam stories, culture, growth

Step 2: Describe your buyer in plain words

Write two or three sentences about the person you want to reach. For example: "Working women in Dhaka, 24 to 35, who buy modest fashion online, compare prices on Facebook and order through Messenger after checking reviews."

That description decides your language, your visuals, your posting times and even your offers. If it reads like everyone, it helps no one.

Step 3: Choose platforms where that buyer already is

  • Facebook: still the main discovery and ordering channel for most local consumer brands.
  • Instagram: strong for fashion, beauty, food, lifestyle and younger, higher-income buyers.
  • TikTok: fast reach for visual products and brands targeting Gen Z.
  • YouTube: best for products that need explanation or demos.
  • LinkedIn: essential for B2B, exporters and hiring.

Start with one main platform and one supporting platform. Add more only when the first ones run smoothly.

Step 4: Build three to five content pillars

Content pillars are the recurring themes you post about. They stop the daily "what should we post today?" panic and keep your feed balanced.

  1. Product and offers: what you sell, prices, new arrivals and deals.
  2. Proof: customer reviews, photos from buyers, results and case stories.
  3. Education: tips, how-tos and answers to common questions.
  4. Behind the scenes: your team, process and the story of the brand.
  5. Community: polls, questions, festivals and local moments.

A useful balance for most small brands: about 40 percent product and offers, 25 percent proof, 20 percent education and 15 percent behind the scenes and community. Adjust it to your goal.

Step 5: Set a posting rhythm you can keep

Consistency beats intensity. A realistic weekly rhythm for a small team might look like this:

Step 6: Decide how you will reply

On social media in Bangladesh, the inbox is where sales happen. Set a reply-time target, prepare saved answers for prices, delivery and payment, and decide who hands leads to the sales team. A slow reply loses the order to whoever answered first.

Step 7: Measure monthly, not daily

Once a month, look at four numbers: reach, engagement, messages or leads, and sales or bookings from social. Then ask two questions: which posts drove the most messages, and what should we make more of next month? Keep the answers in a simple sheet.

A one-page strategy template

  • Goal for the next 90 days: one clear outcome.
  • Buyer: two or three sentences.
  • Platforms: one main, one supporting.
  • Pillars: three to five themes with a rough mix.
  • Rhythm: posts per week, formats and who makes them.
  • Replies: target time, saved answers, lead hand-off.
  • Measurement: four numbers, reviewed monthly.
